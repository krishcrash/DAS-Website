The film is set in the desolate expanses of West Texas in 1980. Llewelyn Moss (Josh Brolin), a local welder and Vietnam War veteran, is out hunting pronghorn when he stumbles upon the aftermath of a botched drug deal. Scattered across the desert are several dead bodies, a dying cartel member, a stash of heroin, and a briefcase containing $2.4 million in cash. Succumbing to temptation, Moss takes the money and flees, triggering a relentless and deadly cat-and-mouse game across the rural landscape.

The cartel quickly realizes the money is missing and hires Anton Chigurh (Javier Bardem), a chilling, sociopathic hitman with an unorthodox weapon—a captive bolt pistol used for cattle—and a chilling worldview. Chigurh tracks Moss with terrifying precision, leaving a trail of bodies in his wake. Meanwhile, Sheriff Ed Tom Bell (Tommy Lee Jones), a weary and aging lawman close to retirement, investigates the escalating violence. Bell struggles to comprehend the modern, senseless nature of the brutality, realizing he is increasingly out of his depth in a changing world.

A Game of Cat and Mouse
Moss attempts to protect his young wife, Carla Jean (Kelly Macdonald), by sending her to stay with her mother while he goes on the run. Despite his survival skills and military background, Chigurh proves nearly impossible to elude, often finding Moss through a tracking device hidden inside the money bag.

Their pursuit leads them across hotels and state lines, culminating in a violent shootout in a Mexican border town that leaves both Moss and several cartel members severely wounded.

An Unforgiving Conclusion
Before the final confrontation can take place on screen, a twist occurs: Moss is ambushed and killed off-camera by Mexican cartel members who have also been tracking the money. When Chigurh arrives shortly after, he recovers the cash, having successfully eliminated his primary target.

In the film’s poignant aftermath, Chigurh visits Carla Jean to fulfill a promise he made to Moss. In a chilling final interaction, he offers her a coin flip to save her life, but she refuses to call it, stating that the choice is up to him. Chigurh kills her and leaves, though he is subsequently involved in a freak car accident that leaves him injured, allowing him to slip away into the anonymous crowd.

The story concludes with Sheriff Bell, now retired, recounting two dreams about his late father to his wife. Reflecting on the encroaching lawlessness and senseless violence of the era, Bell admits that the world has become a place he no longer understands—cementing the film's bleak meditation on aging, morality, and the inescapable nature of evil.
